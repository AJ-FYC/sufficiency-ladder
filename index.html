The Sufficiency Ladder

What happens if most of the profit added by AI goes to the people doing the work, and people who reach "enough" can hand part of their hours to the next person in line? Move the dials and compare four worlds: today's rules, the ladder, a flat dividend paid to everyone, and the ladder with its split taken before costs instead of after.

Assumptions

c. The rest goes to workers. At 100% the ladder pays nothing extra.
Per year for all 30 years, if every profitable AI project is built. 1.5% extends Goldman Sachs's estimate for the decade after adoption; Acemoglu's implies about 0.05%.
λ. 0% means AI only adds output; 100% means it only saves labor.
η. Hours fall as pay per hour rises. 0.2 is roughly what the long-run decline in hours implies (Boppart and Krusell).
ν. 0 keeps "enough" fixed at 25 years of today's living costs; 1 moves it one-for-one with median income.
ψ. Status pressure: the further the top tenth of earners pulls away from the middle, the more people feel they need.

How the model works

Each year, people work, earn, save, and decide how many days to work. AI raises output per hour and may reduce the hours firms need. Under the status quo, owners keep all profit and firms lay off the newest hires when they need fewer hours. On the ladder, profit above today's level is split after costs, the company keeps share c, and workers get the rest per hour worked. Half of each worker's share goes into a personal capital account, and anyone waiting for work draws half a share. People cut hours as their pay rises and as they approach "enough", and the hours they give up go to the next person in line. The gross split is identical except that the company keeps c of the gain before costs and pays for AI itself, so far fewer AI projects pay off for it.

This is a stylized model for comparing mechanisms, not a forecast. It leaves out prices, taxes, trade, capital flight, and differences between firms. Openings that nobody fills, which appear when people choose shorter weeks, lower output; in a real economy, new entrants, migrants or AI could fill them, and who owns that AI matters. Household savings and capital accounts earn fixed returns from outside the model economy.
